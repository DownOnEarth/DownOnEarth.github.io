The feedback delay issue (Online Time Series Forecasting)

Predicting future values in Time Series Forecasting faces an unresolved problem which is that true values (ground truth) are only available after the forecast horizon (how far into the future the prediction is made). Thereby, the model is training on outdated data and may suffer from distribution shifts (especially when the prediction window is large).

Proceed framework (model adapter)

The objective is to adjust the forecasting model’s parameters by estimating the possible concept drift from the current training and test samples, before making the prediction.

Step 1 : Online Fine-Tuning

Given a forecast model FF, take the latest trainable sample (Xt−H,Yt−H)(X_{t-H}, Y_{t-H}) and compute :

Y^t−H=F(Xt−H,θt−H−1)\hat{Y}_{t-H} = F(X_{t-H}, \theta_{t-H-1})

Update model parameters to θt−H\theta_{t-H} using error.

Step 2 : Concept Drift Estimation

Using concept encoders E,E′\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{E}' encode past and current concepts, then calculate the drift :

δt−H→t=ct−ct−H\delta_{t-H \to t} = c_t - c_{t-H}

with:ct=E(Xt)∈Rdcct−H=E′(Dt−)∈Rdcand:Dt−=(Xt−H,Yt−H) \begin{aligned} with : &\\ &c_t = \mathcal{E}(X_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_c} \\ &c_{t-H} = \mathcal{E}'(D_{t-}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_c} \\ \\ and : &\\ &D_{t-} = (X_{t-H},Y_{t-H}) \end{aligned}

Concept encoders

Let Xt−H(i)∈RLX^{(i)}_{t-H} \in \mathbb{R}^L denote the time series of the ii-th variate, given all observations of NN at time tt :

ct−H=E(Dt−)=Average({MLP(Xt−H(i)∣∣Yt−H(i))}i=1N)∈Rdcc_{t-H}=\mathcal{E}(D_{t-})=Average\left(\{ MLP(X^{(i)}_{t-H}||Y^{(i)}_{t-H})\}^{N}_{i=1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_c}

ct=E′(Xt)=Average({MLP′(Xt(i))}i=1N)∈Rdcc_t=\mathcal{E}'(X_t)=Average\left(\{MLP'(X^{(i)}_t)\}^{N}_{i=1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_c}

Where MLPMLP extracts dcd_c latent features from each univariate time series, and AverageAverage yields the average latent features as a global concept that affects all variates. Note also that E′\mathcal{E}' has the potential to estimate the hidden state of YtY_t, if LL (lookback window) is considerably larger than HH (horizon window), XtX_t contains a sequence of horizon windows. The MLP′MLP' can exploit the temporal evolution pattern across horizon windows, and extrapolate the hidden state of the next horizon window.

Step 3 : Proactive model Adaptation

Given an estimated concept drift δt−H→t\delta_{t-H \to t} and the parameters θt−H\theta_{t-H}, employ an adaptation generator GG (one generator module with layer-specific parameters notated G(ℓ)G^{(\ell)}) to generate parameter shifts Δθ\Delta\theta, adjusting θt−H\theta_{t-H} into θ^t\hat{\theta}_t. It is assumed that there exists a learnable relationship between movement in concept space and movement in parameter space.

For each layer ℓ\ell, the shifted parameters are calculated as (with ⊙\odot the element wise multiplication) :

θ^t(ℓ)=(αt(ℓ)⊤βt(ℓ))⊙θt−H(ℓ)\hat{\theta}_t^{(\ell)}=\left({\alpha_t^{(\ell)}}^\top\beta_t^{(\ell)}\right)\odot\theta_{t-H}^{(\ell)}

Where αt(ℓ)\alpha_t^{(\ell)} and βt(ℓ)\beta_t^{(\ell)} are adaptation coefficients generated by G(ℓ)G^{(\ell)} as :

[αt(ℓ),βt(ℓ)]=W2(ℓ)⊤(σ(W1(ℓ)⊤δt−H→t+b(ℓ)))+1=G(ℓ)(δt−H→t)\left[\alpha_t^{(\ell)},\beta_t^{(\ell)}\right]=W_2^{(\ell)\top}\left( \sigma\left(W_1^{(\ell)\top}\delta_{t-H \to t}+b^{(\ell)}\right)\right)+1=G^{(\ell)}(\delta_{t-H\to t})

with:αt(ℓ)∈Rdin, βt(ℓ)∈RdoutW1(ℓ)∈Rr×dc, W2(ℓ)∈R(din+dout)×rb(ℓ)∈Rrand:r≪min(din,dout) a small hyper-parameterσ the sigmoid activation function \begin{aligned} with : &\\ & \alpha_t^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{in}},\ \beta_t^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{out}} \\ & W_1^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{r\times d_c},\ W_2^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{(d_{in}+d_{out})\times r} \\ & b^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^r \\ \\ and : &\\ & r \ll min(d_{in},d_{out}) \ \textit{a small hyper-parameter} \\ & \sigma \ \textit{the sigmoid activation function} \end{aligned}

The adaptation coefficients represent the importance of input-side (α)(\alpha) and output-side (β)(\beta) dimensions of each layer of the forecasting model FF. The multiplication αt(ℓ)⊤βt(ℓ){\alpha_t^{(\ell)}}^\top\beta_t^{(\ell)} permits to create a matrix Mdin×doutM^{d_{in}\times d_{out}} used to update the weights of Ft(ℓ)F^{(\ell)}_t.

To obtain such scaling coefficients, the computed concept drift δt−H→t\delta_{t-H \to t} is fed into each generator G(l)G^{(l)}. The parameters W1(ℓ),W2(ℓ)W_1^{(\ell)}, W_2^{(\ell)} and b(ℓ)b^{(\ell)} are learned, however to further reduce their number W1(ℓ),W2(ℓ)W_1^{(\ell)}, W_2^{(\ell)} are shared across layers of the same type. The bias term b(ℓ)b^{(\ell)} is used to customize distinct coefficient for each layer. Bottleneck layers (shrinkage to size rr with W1(ℓ)W_1^{(\ell)} and expansion to scaling vectors with W2(ℓ)W_2^{(\ell)}) are used to force generalization and efficiency of the generator.

The method is also applied for convolutions and bias terms, for a convolution filter θ(ℓ)∈Rdin×dout×dk\theta^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{in}\times d_{out}\times d_k} with dkd_k kernels, each kernel is multiplied with αt(ℓ)⊤βt(ℓ){\alpha_t^{(\ell)}}^\top\beta_t^{(\ell)}. For a bias term θ(ℓ)∈Rdout\theta^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{out}}, only βt(ℓ)\beta^{(\ell)}_t is generated.

Finally, the complexity of this generator in O(r(ℓ+dc+din+dout))\mathcal{O}(r(\ell+d_c+d_{in}+d_{out})) instead of O(ℓ×dc×din×dout)\mathcal{O}(\ell \times d_c \times d_{in} \times d_{out}) for a naive mapping function (e.g. linear layer).

Step 4 : Online Forecasting

The adapted model yields predictions Y^t=F(Xt,θ^t)\hat{Y}_t = F(X_t, \hat{\theta}_t). At the next time t+1t+1, the parameters will be reset to θt−H\theta_{t-H}

Datasets

  • ETT (Electricity Transformer Temperature) records seven features of electricity transformers from 2016 to 2018. ETTh1 is hourly collected and ETTm1 is 15-minutely collected.
  • ECL records the hourly electricity consumption of 321 clients from 2012 to 2014.
  • Weather records 21 features of weather, e.g., air temperature and humidity in 2020.
  • Traffic records the hourly road occupancy rates recorded by the sensors of San Francisco freeways from 2015 to 2016.

Problem

Impact of temporal gap on forecasting performance

Three baseline models on five standard datasets, comparing two strategies :

  • Practical : At each time tt, before forecasting YtY_t, predictions on Xt−HX_{t-H} are made and the MSE between the ground truth Yt−HY_{t-H} and the prediction Y^t−H\hat{Y}_{t-H} is used to update the model by one-step gradient descent.
  • Optimal : At each time tt, before forecasting YtY_t, the ground truth Yt−1Y_{t-1} is available without any delay. The previous forecast loss on the last sample Xt−1X_{t-1} (i.e., MSE between Yt−1Y_{t-1} and Y^t−1\hat{Y}_{t-1} ) is calculated to update the model by one-step gradient descent.

Observations :

  • The Practical strategy performs worse than Optimal by an average of 107% on different models, meaning that the forecast models are vulnerable to the concept drift caused by the feedback delay issue.
  • The performance gap becomes more significant as the forecast horizon increases.
  • OneNet performs the best under Optimal strategy.
  • PatchTST has the smaller Δ\DeltaMSE but falls short compared to OneNet (under Optimal strategy), signifying room for improvement.

Results

  • Proceed outperforms the existing online model adaptation methods by 12.5%, 13.6%, 6.7% against FSNet, OneNet, and SOLID++, respectively.
  • TCN lags behind a frozen PatchTST with existing online learning methods. However, when enhanced by Proceed it can outperform the frozen PatchTST and iTransformer in some cases of ETTh2, ETTm1, and Weather datasets. These 3 datasets have relatively more significant concept drift, this indicates that Proceed has the capability of handling the concept drift during online learning.

Operations on Concept Encoders

The average operation is simple yet effective. One possible reason is that all variates are typically assumed to have equal importance during both training and evaluation.

With :

  • Linear transformation : learning of a linear layer that transforms the concatenation of all latent features, i.e., ct=W⊤[ct(0),ct(1),⋅⋅⋅]c_t = W^{\top}[c_t^{(0)}, c_t^{(1)}, \cdot \cdot \cdot ].
  • Weighted sum : learning of an individual weight wi∈Rw_i \in \mathbb{R} for each ii-th variate, and ct=∑iwict(i)c_t = \sum_i w_i c_t^{(i)}.
