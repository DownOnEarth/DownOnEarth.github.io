Groups (and Abelian groups)

A group is a set GG with one operation (∗)(*) satisfying the following rules:

  • Closure

    ∀a,b∈Ga∗b∈G\forall a,b \in G\quad a*b\in G

  • Associativity

    ∀a,b,c∈G(a∗b)∗c=a∗(b∗c)\forall a,b,c \in G\quad(a*b)*c=a*(b*c)

  • Identity

    ∃e∈G,∀a∈G : a∗e=e∗a=a\exists e\in G,\quad \forall a\in G\ :\ a*e=e*a=a

  • Inverse

    ∀a∈G,∃a−1∈G : a∗a−1=a−1∗a=e\forall a\in G,\quad \exists a^{-1}\in G\ :\ a*a^{-1}=a^{-1}*a=e

  • Commutativity (Abelian groups only)

    ∀a,b∈Ga∗b=b∗a\forall a,b \in G\quad a*b=b*a

Rings

A ring is a set RR with two operations, usually (+,×)(+,\times), where (R,+)(R,+) is an Abelian group, the other operation (×)(\times) is associative, and (×)(\times) is distributive over (+)(+). A ring may or may not require e(×)e^{(\times)} depending on convention.

Fields

A field is a set FF with two operations, usually (+,×)(+,\times), where both (F,+)(F,+) and (F∗,×)(F^{*},\times)1 are Abelian groups with identity e(+)=0e^{(+)}=0 and e(×)=1e^{(\times)}=1, respectively. As in a ring, (×)(\times) is distributive over (+)(+).
