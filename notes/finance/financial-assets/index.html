A financial asset is an asset that derives its value from a contractual claim, an ownership right or a financial entitlement, rather than its physical characteristics.

Cash / Bank deposits

  • Cash: physical money, issued by the central bank.
  • Bank deposits: claims against commercial banks, potentially earning interest.

Shares

A share represents partial ownership of a corporation, it may give voting rights and dividends respectively depending on the share class and on company decisions.

Bonds

Bonds are debt securities issued by governments, corporations, or other entities, promising repayment under specified terms. Many pay periodic interest (called coupons); zero-coupon bonds do not. The principal is usually returned at maturity.

Derivatives

Derivatives are a financial contracts whose value depends on an underlying asset, rate, index, or event. They are used for hedging or speculation; positions can have positive, zero, or negative value, creating assets or liabilities.

  • Futures: standardized agreements to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price on a specific future date.
  • Forwards: customized agreements to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price on a future date.
  • Options: buyers pay a premium for the right, without obligation, to buy (call) or sell (put) at a specified price, on or before expiration depending on the contract.
  • Swaps: exchanges of cash flows. A credit default swap (CDS) exchanges premiums for protection against specified credit events.

Investment funds

An investment fund is a pool of investors’ money to purchase a diversified portfolio of assets, managed by professional fund managers. Each investor owns shares of the fund itself rather than in the individual underlying assets.

Structure and trading

  • Open-end mutual funds: issue and redeem shares, typically at the next daily NAV per share1, with applicable fees.
  • Exchange-traded funds (ETFs): shares trade on exchanges throughout the day.
  • Listed closed-end funds (CEFs): shares trade on exchanges; investors generally cannot demand redemption by the fund.

ETF and listed CEF market prices can differ from NAV per share.

Management approach

  • Active: managers select investments to meet an objective, such as outperforming a benchmark.
  • Passive / index funds: seek to track an index; may be mutual funds or ETFs.

Other fund labels

  • Money market funds: invest in short-term, high-quality debt and cash instruments. Relatively low risk, but can lose value and lack bank deposit insurance.
  • Hedge funds: private funds with flexible strategies, typically restricted to eligible investors. May use leverage, short selling, and derivatives; risks and withdrawal restrictions vary.

These labels overlap: an ETF can be a passive equity index fund.
